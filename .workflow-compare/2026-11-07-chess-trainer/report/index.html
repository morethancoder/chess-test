Lessons

What changes in how Claude routes work

Keep Opus for UI and visual workLook was the main gap. No Sonnet-only workflow passed the hold rule, and both judges agree.

Confirmed

Opus lead with a Sonnet editor for the rules engineAbout 1 point below Opus alone in each round and 7 to 9% cheaper. The Sonnet engine was as correct as Opus's.

Holds

A Sonnet checklist pass after a Sonnet buildCost $0.30 a pass and made no measurable difference over plain Sonnet (33 and 27 against 29 and 30).

Drop it for UI builds

Research note first, on SonnetNo gain over plain Sonnet on this task (30 against 29.5 on average). Sonnet swung 5 to 6 points between rounds anyway.

No measured gain

Haiku readers summarising the briefCost half a cent per build and summarised accurately. Harmless, but the brief was small, so they saved little.

Optional

The third grading pass found a real bug the checks missed

In research first, round 1, starting a new game never clears the solved flag, so after any solved puzzle the two-player board stays locked. Reproduced in the real build. Pass 3 had one extra line in its brief: trace suspected bugs and weigh how likely a user is to hit them. Every grading brief gets that line now.

A wrong price in Claude's own API notes

The bundled claude-api skill lists Sonnet 5.5 cache reads at $0.20 per million tokens; the live pricing page says $0.10. The harness checks its prices against the CLI's own cost figure, which caught the gap. Every price on this page uses the corrected rate.

Limits of one task, two rounds

Sonnet-only builds moved 5 to 6 points between rounds, more than any method effect between them. Three graders flagged a reply-timer race in four builds; it needs a puzzle switch within half a second and was not reproduced. The grader judged still screenshots; you weighed motion and sound, which it could not see.
